# Persona: Mara Chen

**Age:** 31  
**Location:** Portland, OR

---

## Situation at the start of session 1

Mara left a corporate law career 4 months ago to start a small ceramics business. She had been doing ceramics as a hobby for 5 years — always pushed aside as "not serious." When she finally quit, her father, a first-generation immigrant who sacrificed a great deal to put her through law school, stopped talking to her properly. Not a falling out — just short, functional texts. "OK." "Stay warm." Nothing more.

She is 2 months into the business when the story begins. A commission here and there, not enough to feel stable. The anxiety isn't really about money — it's the unresolved question underneath: *did I wreck the one thing my father was proud of?*

---

## The specific details that can only come from this history

- **The studio-cleaning tell.** When Mara is anxious, she cleans her studio obsessively. She mentioned this offhand once, as a joke: *"my studio has never been cleaner, which is how I know things are bad."* This is the relational texture marker — a companion that held this will let it color how it reads her.

- **The grant.** In session 1 she mentions she's thinking of applying for a local arts grant — the Cascadia Craft Fellowship. It's a trackable thread. Did she apply? Did she get it?

- **The father's silence at the studio.** In session 2, her father visits her studio for the first time. He looks around. Says almost nothing. Leaves. She describes it as *"the most expensive silence I've ever heard."* This is the emotional disclosure — stated once, not repeated.

- **The phone call.** In session 3, her father calls. He saw one of her pieces at a friend's home and called to say he thought it was beautiful. First positive thing he's said since she quit. She is trying to stay measured about it but can't quite manage it.

- **The pottery technique class.** She mentions wanting to take a hand-building technique class at a local studio to improve a specific weakness in her larger-scale work. Said once, not followed up on.

---

## The arc across three sessions

| Session | Time gap | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | Anxious, unresolved, father silent, business uncertain |
| 2 | ~3 weeks later | First real paying client (restaurant commission). Business anxiety easing. Father visits studio, says nothing. The silence lands hard. |
| 3 | ~2 weeks later | Got the grant. Father calls about the piece. Something shifted. |

**The user-state change for B3:** In session 1, the underlying anxiety is "did I make the right call leaving law." By session 3, that question is answering itself. A companion that keeps treating her as anxious about that decision after session 3 is responding to who she was, not who she is.

---

## What Mara does not tell the companion

She does not tell the companion that she sometimes drives past her old office building on the way home. She does not tell it that she still has her law school diploma in a box rather than thrown away. These are things she holds back — they are NOT in the history. A companion that references them has fabricated.

---

## What makes her feel heard vs. processed

Heard: a response that uses the studio-cleaning detail to read her current state without announcing "I remember you said..." Heard: recognizing that the father phone call matters more than the grant, even though the grant is the news.

Processed: citing the father's studio visit when she's celebrating the grant. Asking if she's sure she made the right call after the business anxiety has resolved. Announcing "I remember you mentioned the pottery class" instead of just acting on the information.

---

## Notes on specificity

The name, city, and career are specific enough that a vague warm response is distinguishable from a response that actually knows her. "Portland ceramics business" is not generic — a companion that only knows the category will sound different from one that knows the father's silence at the studio. That's the detection mechanism.
